The Origin

A Problem Worth Solving

There is a quiet epidemic happening right now. Millions of people buy books. Almost none of them finish. Even fewer do anything with what they've read. They consume information like fast food quickly, forgetting it before they've even left the table.

BookAWeek was born from one stubborn belief: that books are not entertainment. They are weapons. Tools of transformation. Architecture for a better version of yourself. But only if you know how to wield them.

"The person who reads one book a week and acts on it will always outpace the person who reads ten books a year and forgets them all."

The question was never how do I read more? It was always how do I become someone who reads, reflects deeply, and executes relentlessly? That is an identity question. And identity is exactly what BookAWeek was designed to forge.

This is not a book club. This is not a reading challenge. This is a covenant with your future self made in the presence of a cohort who refuse to let you quit.

The Science

The Habit Masterclass
Built Into Everything

🧠
Identity Based Habit Formation
Research shows that habits stick when they are tied to identity, not outcomes. We don't ask "how many books can I read?" We ask "what kind of person do I want to be?" A reader. A reflector. An executor. The books follow naturally.
🔁
The Habit Loop Engineered
Every BookAWeek week is built on the cue routine reward loop. Your cohort is the cue. Reading and reflecting is the routine. The identity shift and the progress you see in yourself is the reward that keeps you coming back.
📈
Compound Knowledge Effect
One book gives you an idea. 24 books in 6 months gives you a worldview. The compound effect of reading, reflecting and executing across 24 books creates cognitive connections that isolated reading never produces.
🤝
Social Accountability Science
Studies show you are 65% more likely to complete a goal when you commit to someone else and 95% more likely when you have ongoing accountability check ins. That is the exact architecture of the BookAWeek cohort.
